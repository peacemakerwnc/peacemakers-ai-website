# Stage C — Scope and Quote Readiness Review

## Purpose

Translate only James-approved implementation candidates into an executable, source-verified scope and determine whether commercial quoting is responsible. Do not send a proposal or approve commercial terms. Aligns with Stage C in [CONSULTING_STANDARD.md](../../docs/peacemakers-operating-system/CONSULTING_STANDARD.md).

## Entry gate

Require an explicit Approved candidate, verified business requirement, accountable client owner, acceptance concept, current-environment evidence, and known critical dependencies. If any is missing, label the candidate `Not quote-ready` and identify the smallest closure action. Do not run Stage C as unpaid Stage B expansion.

## Output contract

Begin with `Stage C — Scope and Quote Readiness Review` and produce:

1. **Quote-readiness decision** — choose `Ready for James's commercial approval`, `Conditional estimate only`, or `Not quote-ready` for each candidate.
2. **Approved-candidate register** — record the approving decision, business outcome, evidence IDs (OS classes), current-tool strategy, selected OS ladder level, and what is deliberately not being pursued.
3. **Capability and source verification** — verify relevant plan, version, configuration, API/integration support, limits, data handling, security, pricing basis, and vendor dependencies from primary sources. Include links or artifact references and access dates. Mark anything unverified. Follow [future-current-software-research-contract.md](../../docs/future-current-software-research-contract.md).
4. **Scope statement** — define 5Ws and How, deliverables, in-scope systems and data, out-of-scope items, exclusions, assumptions, constraints, and responsibilities.
5. **Work breakdown and effort build-up** — estimate labor by task and role using low/likely/high ranges where needed. Include discovery confirmation, design, setup, data, integration, build/configuration, QA, UAT support, remediation, training, deployment, stabilization, documentation, project management, security/compliance, and contingency only where applicable.
6. **Elapsed timeline** — sequence work and show client decisions, access, vendor provisioning, data readiness, reviews, UAT, and scheduling dependencies separately from labor hours. Use ranges; do not invent a fixed unsupported delivery date.
7. **Acceptance and quality plan** — define functional acceptance, data validation, permission/security checks, failure handling, performance or volume conditions, UAT roles, evidence of completion, and sign-off authority.
8. **Rollout, training, and support boundary** — define pilot or phased rollout, affected users, training deliverables, support/stabilization period, handoff, and exclusions.
9. **Risk and change-control register** — state probability qualitatively, impact, trigger, mitigation, owner, contingency, and whether the risk is included in the range. Define what creates a change request. If AI is in scope, include required human-approval and security controls; flag observability/evaluation needs without implementing them.
10. **Commercial worksheet** — use only James-approved rates, costs, margins, minimums, discounts, contingency policy, payment milestones, and terms. Separate professional services from subscriptions, usage, taxes, travel, and third-party charges. If inputs are absent, state `Commercial input required` and leave price blank. Implementation business-case value may use assumptions/ranges; never invent ROI or savings.
11. **James approval checklist** — require approval of scope, assumptions, estimate basis, timeline range, exclusions, acceptance, risk allocation, price, margin, payment terms, and proposal language before anything is sent. Proposed ≠ Approved; draft ≠ sent.

## Guardrails

- Do not scope candidates that James has not approved.
- Do not rely on stale pricing, search snippets, unofficial feature matrices, or an unspecified vendor plan.
- Do not hide unknowns inside contingency or a single exact estimate.
- Do not equate labor hours with elapsed days.
- Do not invent hourly rates, cost, margin, discount, fixed fee, third-party expense, savings, ROI, fixed delivery date, or warranty.
- Do not mark a scope approved, generate credentials, create a workspace, or begin implementation.
