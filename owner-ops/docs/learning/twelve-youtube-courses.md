# Twelve practices, one loop

A course for James, taken from the 8 October 2026 carousel by @startup_rules, "12 YouTube courses that are better than an MBA."

Each module names the video, the move worth keeping, a practice on real Peacemakers work or on your own week, and the line you do not cross. The operating system in `owner-ops/docs/peacemakers-operating-system/` stays in charge. These videos do not replace it.

When a module is done, score it with the loop in `owner-ops/skills/owner-practice-loop/`. Watching is the start. The log entry is the finish.

## How a week of this works

1. Read one module.
2. Watch that video, or the chapter the module names if the video is several hours.
3. Do the practice on the next real task of that kind.
4. Write one line in `practice-log.md`: used, skipped, unclear, boundary, or harm.
5. If the card was unclear or would have broken a rule, tighten it before you use it again.

Start at module 1. Money, focus, and life stay in your personal notes. Sales, negotiation, and automation produce notes for you to decide. They do not send anything.

## Module 1. See the system before you move

**Video:** Sandeep Swadia, [How To Think SO Clearly People Assume You're Brilliant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mjTgkm-h__M) (`systems`)

Sandeep's useful move is to name the kind of system before choosing a response.

- **Clear.** Cause and effect are obvious. Use the checklist. The brown M&M in the Van Halen contract is a signal that the rest of the checklist was skipped.
- **Complicated.** A cause exists and an expert can find it. Slow down. Get the right specialist, not a nearby one.
- **Complex.** Cause and effect show up afterward. Culture, adoption, and raising a teenager sit here. Run a small test and course-correct. A roadmap does not make adoption clear.
- **Chaotic.** The link is broken and still moving. Stabilize first. The Tylenol poisonings are his example: pull the product, warn people, understand later.

Three traps sit underneath. You can reward the wrong behavior and get more of it, as in the cobra bounty. The result of today's action can arrive years later. From inside the system you cannot tell whether your train is moving. Mentors, data, and time are how you stand on the platform.

His diagnostic is DART: deconstruct the parts, analyze how visible cause and effect is, recognize a pattern you have seen, test the smallest response. In a chaotic system there is no time to test.

**Practice.** On the next process you touch, write four lines: parts, connection, repeating pattern, system type. Then do only the matching response.

**Leave behind.** Cleverness in a clear system. A checklist for a complex adoption problem. Analysis while something is still unsafe.

## Module 2. Study a domain until you can say it back

**Video:** Andrew Huberman's study guidance. Slide clock 10:49 matches [this upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jMhhaAQK1NQ) (`study`). Long form: [Optimal Protocols for Studying & Learning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ddq8JIMhz7c).

The habits that carry the result:

- Put the session on the calendar, two or three blocks, and repeat the time until attention arrives with it.
- Study alone. Phone in another room.
- Recall with the source closed. The miss is the lesson. Checking afterward is part of the method.
- Teach the idea in your own words. The gap shows up when you have to say it.
- Sleep after the session. That is when the change sticks.

**Practice.** Pick one standard you rely on, such as the evidence classes or the intervention ladder. Two blocks. Second block is closed-book. Then explain it once without the page.

**Leave behind.** Rereading, highlighting, and a second screen.

## Module 3. Speak the point you can support

**Video:** Natalie Dawson, [How To Speak Like The 1% Elite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VY0r0WV0GAQ) (`communication`)

Five skills, in her order:

1. Speak to lead. "I think" and "I feel" shrink a statement you already mean. Say the path and the result.
2. Let emotion pass through. Her picture is grass, not a toothpick. The rock falls through and the grass stands back up. Then answer.
3. Know why you are in the room. If you have no target, leave. Weight comes from being in the right conversation.
4. Ask the question that finds the knot. "What data supports this?" "What is the biggest constraint?" "What would make this never happen again?"
5. Say it once. Over-explaining is a request to be liked. Sit still, shoulders back, volume up when you notice yourself getting quiet.

**Practice.** Before the next call, write why you are there. In the notes afterward, mark one qualifier you can delete because the evidence already supports the sentence, and one question you still need answered.

**Leave behind.** Certainty you have not earned. A calm tone on top of an unsourced claim. Peacemakers labels that claim with its evidence class.

## Module 4. Put the listener in the scene

**Video:** Philipp Humm, [Give me 18min & I'll improve your storytelling skills by 183%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YtkrIaONxu0) (`storytelling`)

The 183 percent is a title. The method is PAST. Zoom into the moment instead of summarizing it from above.

- **Place.** Time and location, one line. "Last July, I'm standing outside the conference room."
- **Action.** Verbs. You walk, open, stop.
- **Speech.** The exact line that turned the scene. If you do not remember it, use words that could have been said, and do not present them as a transcript of a client.
- **Thoughts.** The raw thought, not the polished one. Then pause on the line that matters.

**Practice.** Rewrite one explanation you are about to give so it contains a place, an action, one line of speech, and one thought. The lesson after the scene may not add a fact.

**Leave behind.** Invented client results. A story doing the work of the evidence register.

## Module 5. Sell the outcome, then stop

**Video:** Alex Hormozi, [How to Sell Better than 99% Of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE2_7elAcxM) (`sales`)

Watch the closing-framework section, not all four hours, the first time. He teaches CLOSER from scripts he compared:

- **C.** Clarify why they took the call and what they want solved.
- **L.** Label the problem in their words and let them own it.
- **O.** Overview what they already tried, for how long, and how it went.
- **S.** Sell the vacation, not the plane flight. Questions, restatements, and short stories. Three pillars. A few minutes, not a tour of modules.
- **E.** Explain the concern they actually raised.
- **R.** Reinforce the decision afterward, because the relationship keeps asking them to do the hard thing.

He also says the only lines on the call are questions, restatements, and stories, and that being understood does more than the pitch.

**Practice.** On the next discovery conversation, fill C, L, and O in their words before you name a tool. Describe the business result. Hand E and R to yourself as the owner. Price and scope wait for your approval.

**Leave behind.** Pressure, income claims, "it cannot lose," and any sentence a skill would send to a client. The four-hour tape is a sales-team training. Peacemakers sells a careful recommendation, and only after you approve it.

## Module 6. Make a demand, and leave them a way to agree

**Video:** Matthias Schranner, [Negotiation skills for life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DpmvQm1PMPk) (`negotiation`). TEDxZurich.

His police examples are there to show the shape of a stuck conflict. The transferable moves:

- An argument invites a counter-argument. A demand can be traded. "You are wrong" ends the talk.
- Under pressure, flight offers a weak compromise or escalates to a boss. Fight talks too much. Staying is the third move.
- His line is: "Difficult." Pause. "Thank you for this opening." Name the shared interest. Then make the demand.
- At the end, give the other side a way to agree without losing standing. The firefighter jacket in his suicide story is that idea. The jacket is the idea. The story is not a script.

**Practice.** When a scope or price conversation sticks, write the shared interest, one demand, and one face-saving option. Decide it yourself.

**Leave behind.** Threats, bluffs, and giving away an approval gate to finish the meeting.

## Module 7. Protect the first block

**Video:** Rian Doris, [How To Unlock Insane Focus On Command](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l86xggdQcKQ) (`focus`)

Four pillars from the flow research he and Steven Kotler teach:

- **Blockers.** The phone in the first minutes of the day is the dam. Flow before phone: two or three hours on the main task.
- **Proneness.** The first part of the morning is the easiest entry, because sleep-like brain states are still close and the world has not booked you yet.
- **Triggers.** A clear goal, immediate feedback, and work a little harder than your current skill. About four percent harder is his number for the edge between boredom and anxiety.
- **Cycle.** Struggle, release, flow, recovery. People quit in the opening minutes of struggle, while cortisol and norepinephrine are doing their job. Stretch the attention by one more minute, the way you would add a rep.

**Practice.** Tomorrow, write the goal and the feedback in one line. Start that task before the phone. Stay through the squirm. Stop for recovery instead of filling the rest of the day with more struggle.

**Leave behind.** Using "flow" as a reason to skip sleep or a review.

## Module 8. Decide what you will not do

**Video:** Dan Martell, [How to Be So Productive it Feels ILLEGAL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hSGt_rhu49U) (`productivity`)

Ten hacks. Keep these:

1. A not-to-do list: low-value work, vices, people who drain you. A yes is a no to the main thing.
2. Focus triggers: a chair, a sound without lyrics, a start routine.
3. Hard mode first. One domino, in a deep-work block, before email.
4. Easy to find, hard to reach. Notifications off. A scheduled time to reply.
5. A shorter deadline than the one you would give yourself.
6. Teach the algorithm by watching what you want more of, then teach it to someone.
7. A week built from the big rocks, themed days, and your energy, with similar tasks batched.
8. A visible streak or milestone.
9. Move your body when the mind is loud. Energy is created, then spent.
10. Review the calendar and drop what no longer fits.

**Practice.** This week's not-to-do list, three lines. The hardest owner task goes in the first block. Log whether it happened before Slack.

**Leave behind.** His example of a job depending on a weight goal. That is not how this company works. Also leave behind a new app. The list and the block come first.

## Module 9. Climb Claude one level at a time

**Video:** Metics Media, [The Ultimate Beginner's Guide to Claude AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9oJySubZRSA) (`claude`)

Matt's ladder, applied to your work rather than his candle shop:

1. **Talk.** Context, task, and format. Refine in the same thread. A new topic gets a new thread.
2. **Hand over the world.** Files and pages for this task. Same task, same chat.
3. **Make the thing.** A document, a sheet, a draft you can edit.
4. **Memory.** Only for stable preferences. Client facts do not go into a standing memory.
5. **Connect apps you already use.** One folder, one boring job, and you can see what it may touch.
6. **Work while you are away.** Only after levels 1 through 5 are dull, and only with an approval step and a log.

He also says the default model is for ordinary work, a stronger one is for long or delicate work, and a faster one is for many small questions. The names on screen (he mentions Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, and Fable) will change. Follow the job, not the screenshot. Start on the free plan until the limit is the thing in your way. Turn off "train on my chats" for any work that includes client material.

**Practice.** Mark today's task with a level from 1 to 6. Do it at that level. Write the three-part prompt. Refine twice before you open a new chat.

**Leave behind.** An unattended run against email, files, or payments. Copying his example prompts.

## Module 10. Automate the step a rule can already do

**Video:** Nick Saraev, [N8N FULL COURSE 6 HOURS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2GZ2SNXWK-c) (`automation`)

Chapters on the video itself:

- 00:01:25 n8n basics
- 01:11:41 foundational concepts
- 03:11:09 JavaScript functions
- 05:04:47 self-hosting
- 05:28:07 n8n compared with Make.com

Under those headings he builds triggers, forms, HTTP and database nodes, branching, and later AI agents with memory and tools. He also shows how people package workflows as a service. Examples he has described publicly include a booking reply that logs to a CRM, a draft of a personalized email, and a filter that pre-drafts a reply.

**Practice.** Pick one internal chore you repeat. Walk eliminate, simplify, configure, then connect. Open n8n only if those rungs fail. The first workflow is deterministic, writes a log, and waits for you before it does anything a client could see. Study the Make.com comparison before you host anything.

**Leave behind.** Selling automations from this module. Cold email. An LLM node where a rule works. A self-hosted tutorial pointed at production.

## Module 11. Split your own paycheck on purpose

**Video:** Mark Tilbury, [How To Manage Your Money Like The 1%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QThz1B8SHmc) (`money`)

This module is personal. It does not go in a client deliverable, and it is not advice from Peacemakers. Tilbury says he is not a financial adviser. Part of the video is sponsored. The 10 percent return is his illustration, using a long-run index average he quotes. Verify any account, tax wrapper, or rate yourself before you use it.

His split of income:

- **25% growth.** Assets and skills. He starts people with broad index funds, accumulation units so dividends reinvest, and an automatic payday transfer. Stock picking and alternatives are the small slice, if any.
- **15% stability.** About five months of baseline living costs. Easy to reach within a day, not invested, earning ordinary savings interest. If you spend it, you replace it on the next paycheck.
- **50% essentials.** Housing, food, utilities, transport, insurance, the clothes you actually wear. He uses the cap to force lifestyle creep into the open.
- **10% rewards.** A separate balance for trips, hobbies, people, and gifts. When it is empty, you wait. He spends this slice on experiences.

**Practice.** Write your monthly baseline. Multiply by five. That number is the stability target. If you adopt the split, automate the 25 and the 10 on payday.

**Leave behind.** Putting the stability fund in the market. Handing this split to a client. Treating the sponsor segment as research.

## Module 12. Keep fewer desires, and correct yourself

**Video:** Chris Williamson with Naval Ravikant, [44 Harsh Truths About The Game Of Life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KyfUysrNaco) (`life`)

The title says 44. The moves worth keeping for how you run your week:

- Desire is a contract to be unhappy until you get the thing. Keep few.
- On an optional commitment, "if you cannot decide, the answer is no." A four-year commitment deserves a long look. A perishable spark gets acted on while it is alive, or it is dropped.
- Pride is expensive because it blocks the correction. Mastery is ten thousand corrections, not ten thousand unmarked hours.
- Do the work that feels like play to you and looks like work to other people.
- Stress is two wants pulling on the same beam. Drop one.
- Win a game so you can be free of it. Fix the life in front of you before you take on a distant cause.
- A calendar full of obligations your past self accepted is a tax on the day you are actually having.

**Practice.** This week, decline one optional thing you cannot decide. Name one desire you are willing to keep. Make one correction you noticed in the work.

**Leave behind.** Using "the answer is no" on a client decision the evidence can settle. Putting these sentences into a proposal. Filling the calendar to look busy.

## The loop, in one pass

After any module:

1. **Observe.** Which card, which live task, which rule binds it.
2. **Practice.** Do that card once.
3. **Measure.** used, skipped, unclear, boundary, or harm, plus one sentence.
4. **Revise.** Leave a card that worked. Clarify a card that confused you. Tighten a card that would have broken a rule.

Then the next module. The skill `owner-practice-loop` runs the same four steps and is allowed to edit a card when the log shows it failed. It is not allowed to email a client, send a price, or start an unattended agent.
