01

Executive summary

Charter wants smoother work, satisfied clients, and a reliable path for a standard home: about eight months from footings in the ground to closing, after the pre-construction period, with custom footprints sometimes closer to ten months. The purchase-order and invoice process is the same on both.

This Blueprint is based on Charter’s questionnaire and the 6 October 2026 process review with Holly Overton, Anne Cooley, and Angie Valentine. Buildertrend selections were walked live. The Sage entry path was not. On 7 October 2026 Anne emailed a process packet (tile templates and a completed tile sheet, two electrical option templates and a completed electrical sheet, and a completed interior paint sheet). Angie was copied. That packet is enough to start schema work on tile and electrical. A blank paint template was not in the set. The Riverlights (or other) ARC form still needs to be confirmed if it was not attached.

Central conclusion. Start with designer worksheets so Charter can hand a client a consistent packet without walking the full Buildertrend selection tree. Keep Buildertrend as the system for jobs, schedules, selections, and formal approval. Keep Sage 100 Contractor as the accounting and job-cost system. Do not replace either one.

Recommended direction (Proposed): Phase 1 designer worksheets (tile and ARC first, then paint and electrical on that pattern) → Phase 2 purchase order, invoice, and Sage, designed end to end before any build → Phase 3 reporting later.

Attention now

Give designers one Charter format for tile and exterior packets, then paint and electrical. Anne keeps formal entry and approval in Buildertrend. People review anything a client will see.

Not yet

Replacing Buildertrend or Sage, buying a broad Sage add-on suite, restarting a stalled Smartsheet PO file, auto-writing selections into Buildertrend, or a reporting dashboard.

03

Priority workflow findings

Designer tile packaging

What we confirmed

On the call, tile packaging ran as a chain: the designer meets the client and writes selections, those notes go into an Excel tile sheet, a visual palette is often made one item at a time in a general chat tool, and Anne then enters each item into Buildertrend, including images. A live job showed room-by-room tile (baths, laundry, fireplace, kitchen backsplash options, sunroom) and more than one option for the same location. Charter described designer time for that tile path as roughly a few hours, plus about half an hour for Anne’s Buildertrend entry. That figure was discussed, not measured with a time study. The 7 October packet includes a tile template and a completed HLRL9017 tile sheet, which is enough to draft a field list.

Where it breaks down

The same facts are retyped. Palettes change with the chat. Clients and staff cannot pull a clean “this bathroom” packet from the full selection tree. Buildertrend’s export on the call was a printable PDF, not a working spreadsheet.

Why it matters

This is the repeating tax on every home, and it is the win Anne named for this year: worksheets people will actually use. Soft review should not require the full Buildertrend tree. Formal approval still should.

What remains unresolved

Final column freeze against the template and the HLRL9017 example. Whether the designer photo and stock library was included beyond the sheets named above. Who signs the soft-review PDF versus the Buildertrend approval.

ARC / exterior submittal

What we confirmed

Exterior colors and materials (siding, brick, roof, shutters, door, trim, and related items) are chosen, then copied from Buildertrend into a Word, PowerPoint, or Publisher packet. An ARC form, including the Riverlights DRC form discussed on the call, is filled by hand.

Where it breaks down

It is the same assemble-by-hand pattern as tile, on a form a reviewer outside Charter will read. A missed field shows up late.

Why it matters

Tile and ARC were the two starters the call agreed to build first, because the look and feel then carries to paint, electrical, flooring, hardware, and appliances.

What remains unresolved

The 7 October attachment list did not name an ARC or Riverlights form. If that form was not in the packet, the first ARC worksheet still needs the form version Charter wants frozen. Other communities’ forms are a later decision, not part of the first pattern.

Paint and electrical options

What we confirmed

Paint follows the package-then-upload pattern and is not identical to tile’s line-by-line Buildertrend entry. The 7 October packet includes a completed HLRL9017 interior paint sheet and does not include a blank paint template. Electrical is a connected-spreadsheet problem: a master price list with a client price and an electrician price, a job sheet of the items that apply, and two views. Today the second view is made by deleting columns. Buildertrend receives a marked-up plan PDF and totals, not the full line list. Anne sent two electrical option templates and the HLRL9017 electrical options sheet.

Where it breaks down

Paint still depends on a clear blank structure. Electrical depends on keeping the client from seeing the electrician column, without maintaining two unrelated files.

Why it matters

Angie and the room aligned on worksheets first. Paint and electrical are the same Phase 1 family, sequenced after the tile and ARC pattern so Charter is not buying a separate tool for each category.

What remains unresolved

A blank paint template, or Anne’s confirmation that the completed sheet’s columns are the template. Confirmation that the two electrical templates are the client view and the electrician view. Pricing remains a human step.

Purchase order → invoice → Sage

What we confirmed

Two paths run in parallel. Supers handwrite paper POs for raw materials. Those paper POs are not in Buildertrend; the intended record is Sage. Buildertrend POs cover selections and subcontractors, and supers approve labor before an invoice is approved there. Charter reported that about 95 to 99 percent of invoices arrive at a dedicated email address, with a small share by mail that is printed, stamped, and coded. Matching is manual. There is no in-house purchaser leading orders. A recent field role helps with purchasing; a dedicated purchasing manager was not described as required at current volume. Tablets exist. A SharePoint digital-PO pilot was started and not finished. Charter also reported that a Smartsheet PO path has stalled. Sage 100 Contractor stays. Charter has already looked at broader Sage add-ons, including Construction Management and Paperless-style options, and described them as too expensive for the one capability needed.

Where it breaks down

Paper has no digital destination that makes matching worth the change in habit. Nothing ties the paper PO to Sage. Anne’s point on the call: do not put a temporary fix on matching if that fix has to be thrown away when Sage is connected.

Why it matters

This is real operational drag, and it is the wrong first build. The call treated it as the heavier second phase, designed completely, then implemented in increments.

What remains unresolved

The Sage screen-share (one unpaid invoice, a paper PO match, the Buildertrend approval queue, and the Sage entry steps). Monthly PO and invoice counts, first-match failures, and days from invoice received to Sage entry. Which PO is authoritative before Sage. Those gauges were requested and not captured.

All-jobs reporting

What we confirmed

The live schedule sits in Buildertrend. A separate Excel history, back to about 2017 and on the order of 230 jobs, carries contract, permit, footing, target and revised closing, and variance. Anne fills milestone dates by checking photos, daily logs, and the schedule. Buildertrend was described as having no custom report builder for that view. Permit timing was described as living in the workbook rather than as a date field in Buildertrend.

Where it breaks down

Holly, Anne, and Angie cannot treat one screen as the shared milestone picture. The workbook and Buildertrend can drift.

Why it matters

It is a visibility problem. It is also a poor place to start, because the worksheet and PO paths are where staff time is leaking now.

What remains unresolved

Which milestones must be fields versus a periodic export. Whether a later connector is justified. No reporting build is proposed now.

04

Root-cause themes

One set of facts, several handmade copies

Tile, paint, exterior, and electrical each collect the decision once, then rebuild it for the client, the vendor, the ARC reviewer, and Buildertrend. The delay is reassembly, not a missing brand of software.

Buildertrend is the approval record, and a weak soft-review packet

Clients approve in Buildertrend, and that control should stay. Using the full selection tree as the design conversation is what makes packaging slow. Worksheets can sit in front of Buildertrend without becoming a second system of record.

Field orders and office matching were never given one destination

Supers order directly, which matches how Charter builds. Paper and Buildertrend grew up beside each other. Tablets, SharePoint, and Smartsheet have not closed the gap to Sage, so people stay on the path that already matches an invoice.

Ad-hoc chat output is standing in for a Charter format

General chat tools already help make palettes. They do not share a field list, a photo library, or a review rule, so two jobs do not look like the same company.

Still open, and not treated as causes: exact PO volume and fail rate, invoice-to-Sage days, change orders per standard home, and which workbook wins when Excel, Buildertrend, and Sage disagree.

05

Controls to preserve

  • Buildertrend remains the record for jobs, the live schedule, selections, client-facing approval, and selection or subcontractor POs.
  • Sage 100 Contractor remains the accounting and job-cost record. No worksheet project posts to Sage.
  • Formal client approval stays in Buildertrend. A worksheet PDF is for soft review and for Anne’s upload, not a replacement signature path.
  • Human review before a packet goes to a client, an ARC reviewer, or Buildertrend, and before any future invoice suggestion is posted.
  • Electrical price separation. The client view shows the client price. The electrician view can show both. People still price from the master list.
  • Who orders. Superintendents keep ordering. This Blueprint does not assume a new purchasing-manager process.
  • Charter-owned accounts. Licenses, catalogs, job data, and client communications stay Charter’s. Material changes to systems, permissions, or financial records need Charter’s approval before they go live.

Recommended action plan

All items are Proposed. “Later” is not a promise to build.

Now — Phase 1 worksheets

  • Freeze a tile field list from the Tile SS Template and the HLRL9017 tile sheet. Produce a Charter-branded worksheet and a client PDF / palette, including rooms with more than one option.
  • Pair that with one ARC / exterior form version once Charter confirms the form (Riverlights DRC if that is the form to freeze).
  • Then paint and electrical on the same pattern: paint from the completed sheet plus a blank template or a confirmed column list; electrical from the two templates, still human-priced, with the two views generated instead of column-deleted.
  • Pilot on one or two jobs. Anne confirms the upload into Buildertrend. Designers get a short walkthrough.
  • No Buildertrend write-back in this phase.

Next — Phase 2 purchase order, invoice, and Sage

  • Hold a Sage and invoice walkthrough before any build is treated as defined.
  • Write the end-to-end path: digital destination for field material POs, invoice-email capture, match flags, and Sage entry, with a person confirming anything that posts.
  • Implement one increment only after that design. Matching work should still be useful when Sage is connected.
  • Leave SharePoint and Smartsheet as prior attempts, not as the recommended system.

Later — Phase 3 reporting

  • After worksheets are in use, decide whether Buildertrend fields plus an export are enough, or whether a Charter view of milestones is still needed.
  • Additional trade worksheets (flooring, hardware, appliances, and others Anne named) only after the first pattern is accepted.
  • Buildertrend write-back, if ever wanted, is its own decision.

Not currently justified

  • Replacing Buildertrend or Sage 100 Contractor, or moving accounting into Buildertrend.
  • Buying Sage Construction Management, Paperless, or a similar broad add-on for a single step. Charter already judged those packages oversized for the need.
  • Reviving the stalled Smartsheet PO workbook, or another half-finished digital PO silo.
  • A live client portal that replaces Buildertrend approval.
  • Automatic Sage posting, a full accounts-payable rewrite, or an all-jobs dashboard as part of the worksheet work.
07

What we are not recommending — yet

These stay off the table until the evidence changes. They are not permanent bans.

  • A new core system. The friction is packaging, matching, and reporting around tools Charter already runs.
  • Vendor add-on suites as the Phase 2 answer. A thin, approved pull is the direction only after the Sage walk shows what is actually missing. Capability of any connector is unverified until then.
  • Chat-by-chat palettes as the production method. A controlled worksheet can still use assistive drafting. A person reviews the packet before it is sent or uploaded.
  • Industry tools that were only context on the call — marketing drafts, lead follow-up, permitting services, autonomous scheduling, or plan-generation platforms. None of those were selected as Charter’s problem to solve first.

10

Future AI and automation opportunities

These are Potential Future Fit only. They are not the Phase 1 recommendation. Phase 1 is a controlled worksheet. Assistive generation may help inside that worksheet; a person still approves the packet. Nothing below enters a build unless Charter later selects it and the prerequisites are true.

Invoice-email capture and mismatch flags

Potential Future Fit — Phase 2 candidate, not a current build

Use: read invoice PDFs from the dedicated inbox, line them up to a digital PO, and flag mismatches for a person.

Before it is real: Sage walkthrough, a written end-to-end design, and a rule that nothing posts to Sage without a person. A capture tool that ignores Sage is the Band-Aid the call rejected.

Why not now: the entry path is still unseen, and volumes are unknown.

Catalog-bound palette assist

Potential Future Fit — only after the worksheet fields are stable

Use: draft a room palette from the frozen columns and Charter’s own photos, in the same layout every time.

Before it is real: agreed fields, rights to the photo library, and human review. This is an aid inside the worksheet, not a new design platform.

Why not now: the format has to exist first, or the assist will recreate today’s one-off chats.

Buildertrend-native drafting aids

Potential Future Fit — unverified on Charter’s plan

Use: if Charter’s Buildertrend plan already includes client-update drafts or bill capture, those could sit beside Sage later, with a person sending or posting.

Before it is real: confirmation of what the current subscription actually includes. A product name is not evidence.

Why not now: they do not fix the worksheet chain, and bill capture must not bypass the Sage design.
