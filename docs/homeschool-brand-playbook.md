## The decision

**The one thing this account is known for**

A Catholic dad telling the truth about prayer and homeschool in his own house, with his kids off camera.

**Positioning line** (same bio on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube):

> Catholic dad. We homeschool. I talk about the prayers we pray and how we teach. The kids stay off camera.

Practical AI stays out of that line. A tool is not the name of the account.

**The niche.** Catholic parents who homeschool, or who are close to starting, and especially dads who do not see themselves in a polished mom feed. They want an ordinary account of prayer and teaching. They do not need to see the children.

"Catholic homeschool dad using practical AI" is too narrow, and it puts the tool in the title. People who want an AI demo are not the people this account is for. AI can show up later, as a screen recording, when it does one job in the house.

Blair's "drop your niche" does **not** mean this account should also be NetSuite, the Army, and the company. Those stay on their own surfaces, as the personal playbook says. Inside Catholic homeschool, drop the smaller niche. Do not become the account for one curriculum, one rite, one method, or one argument. The niche is this dad, these prayers, and this day. That is specific enough to be recognized and wide enough for a mom, a new homeschool parent, and a dad who already teaches at home.

Who it is for:

- Catholic parents teaching their kids at home.
- Catholic dads who want a man's voice on faith and the school day without a costume.
- A parent deciding, who wants an honest day rather than a sales page.

Who it is not aimed at, and who is still welcome to watch: families who use a parish school or a public school, Protestants, Catholics who homeschool differently, parents who show their kids. They are not the target. They are not the enemy.

**Honest common enemy.** The pressure to perform a finished Catholic homeschool.

Say this: "Our homeschool is not a show, and it is not school from 8 to 3 at the table."

That pressure has two faces he has already refused. One is copying a school day onto the kitchen table. The other is a holy family arranged for the camera. He stands with the parent who is trying to pray and teach in a real house. The hero is that parent, in their own house. He is a dad on a walk. He does not need fans, and he does not rank other parents.

**Charitable limits.** "We don't" is the whole argument. He does not attack other families, public schools, parish schools, hybrid schools, other homeschool methods, other Catholics, or Protestants. He does not score a liturgy, a curriculum, a mom who shows her children, or a dad who chose a classroom. Another Catholic family is not the villain. A Protestant neighbor is not the villain. A school is not the villain. "The devil" is not a marketing enemy. If a card is really about temptation or evil, he says it the way he would say it while leading a prayer, and he does not aim it at a group of people.

What he can say about the faith, and no further:

- Parents are the first teachers of their children. That is an ordinary Catholic sentence. It is not a claim that homeschool is the only faithful choice. The Church also leaves room for school. He says what this family does.
- He names the prayers they pray and says that he leads the rosary. He does not invent a rule that every Catholic homeschool must pray this list.
- A saint is a person who followed Christ. He tells one he can explain to his own kids. He does not invent a private story, a patronage they have not chosen, or a miracle.
- Advent is the season of waiting for Christmas. It starts Sunday 29 November 2026. He describes what this house does when they are doing it. If the house has no Advent practice yet, he says that, and he does not install one for content.

## Her points, on this account

Faith and homeschool get the same answer, because they are one brand. Each block is her point, whether it holds here, and what he does.

### 1. Stop posting content and build a common enemy

**Holds if the enemy is a pressure, not a person.** Name the pressure his viewers already feel: to look like a finished Catholic homeschool, either as an all-day school at the table or as children arranged for the camera.

**Here.** One sentence, repeated: "Our homeschool is not a show, and it is not school from 8 to 3 at the table." Then a true detail from the house. Prayer and the school day belong in the same post when both happened. Do not open a second account for "faith content."

### 2. People want a culture, a community, and a movement, not more content

**Half holds.** Parents stay for a dad they recognize, praying prayers they recognize. He does not need a movement, a membership, or a homeschool camp.

**Here.** The culture is already the house: those four prayers, the rosary he leads, a lesson that stops when focus is gone, kids off camera. He does not announce a community. Two true walks a week will do more than a rally.

### 3. Give them an enemy to band against and a hero to root for

**The hero half holds. A war does not.** The hero is the parent doing their own imperfect day, including parents who do not homeschool.

**Here.** He talks as a dad who leads the rosary and teaches his kids. He does not ask anyone to root for him against a school, a parish, or another Christian. "Band against" is the wrong verb. "This is what we do" is the right one.

### 4. Name what you stand against — hustle culture, the nine-to-five, the devil

**The instruction holds. Her examples do not.** What he stands against is the performed Catholic homeschool. Hustle culture is vague. The nine-to-five fights his actual job and does not belong on this account. "The devil" as a brand villain is irreverent, and it is not something he aims at other people.

**Here.** He can say the day is too staged, the table is too much like a bell schedule, and the camera is the wrong place for his children. He can talk about prayer, the saints, and Advent as things this house keeps. He cannot talk about other Catholics, Protestants, or schools as the thing he is against.

### 5. Cult-like fans who rally to the mission

**Drop.** "Cult-like" is her claim, and it is the wrong aim on a Catholic account. A dad selling a cult, even as a joke, is off. Nothing in the Reel measured those fans.

**Here.** A parent who recognizes their own kitchen is the win. No inside language, no "real Catholics do this," no rally. He wants to be useful and believed.

### 6. Drop your niche

**Holds inside Catholic homeschool. Does not erase the account's subject.** Dropping the niche does not add NetSuite to this bio, and it does not make "practical AI" the niche. It means he refuses a smaller box: one curriculum, one rite, Latin-only, a method war.

**Here.** The positioning line is the niche. Catholic, homeschool, this dad, kids off camera. Faith is not a sub-niche he posts on Sundays only. A walk about the school day can name a prayer. A walk about the rosary can name what they did when focus broke. Same account, same man.

### 7. Pillars around values, beliefs, skills, and controversial takes

**Holds when the takes are ones he already lives.** Manufactured controversy about other Christians is a costume.

**Here.** The pillars are below. The takes he will repeat:

- We pray these prayers, and I lead the rosary.
- We do not run school from 8 to 3 at the table. When focus goes, we play outside.
- I have no teaching certificate. I know my kids.
- The kids never go on camera, including for a holy picture.
- AI is a separate screen recording, and only when it does one job.
- He does not tell other families they are doing faith or school wrong.

### 8. What you are selling, they buy you

**Partly holds, and he is not selling a program.** People stay because they trust the dad. There is no offer on this account. Peacemakers AI prices, the book pitch, and NetSuite do not ride along.

**Here.** He is specific and easy to trust: a Catholic dad, these prayers, this kind of day, children unseen. *Bible Stories to Color* can be mentioned when a real page is the point. He talks about the page, not about a child using it. He does not say "they buy you."

### 9. Forget looking polished

**Holds.** He already films unscripted, from a card, on a walk. A phone is enough. One take is enough if it is true.

**Here.** Do not wait for a feast-day setup, matching theology language, or a thumbnail with the children. Cut a clip that shows a child, a voice that identifies them, or a school detail that points at them. An AI demo is a screen recording made at another time, not a polished insert in the walk.

### 10. Unpolished videos made her 100k in a month

**Drop. Unproven.** The words were not stable across transcription passes, and the figure is not his life. The "be rich" card on that Reel is not a Catholic homeschool line.

**Here.** He does not hint that homeschool content, a book, or an AI tool paid a bill. No follower counts as a flex. A prayer and a school day are not a funnel.

### 11. People connect with real, not with perfect

**Holds.** This is the part of her polish tip worth keeping.

**Here.** Real is a named prayer, a lesson that stopped, a walk in Weaverville, a saint he can explain, an Advent practice they actually keep. Real is not his children's faces, their behavior at Mass, another family's failure, or a struggle he has not decided to tell. If he has not lived it this week, it stays off the card. That includes a saint he only met on a content list, and an Advent wreath they do not own.

### 12. The website matters less than posting

**Holds for a personal site he does not need. Does not shrink the company site.** He does not need a new website for `@buildingpeacemakers` this week. `peacemakersai.com` still explains the Blueprint and stays off these bios.

**Here.** Consistency is two honest walks, posted where he already talks. The book page can be linked when a post is about the book. The company site is not the link in a rosary video.

### 13. Polish is the excuse for never pressing post

**Holds as a diagnosis.** The stall is waiting to sound like a better Catholic or a real teacher.

**Here.** The card is the cure. A few true words, then walk. He still does not press post on a clip with a child in it, a clip that scores another family, or a clip he has not watched. This playbook is not permission to publish. He approves the clip.

### 14. Ignite people's emotions

**The feeling holds. The tactic does not.** Recognition does the work: a dad who prays the Our Father and does not pretend the lesson went perfectly. Anger at schools, fear about "those" Catholics, and a rally are the wrong fuel. They also fail charity.

**Here.** Say the concrete thing and stop. "I lead the rosary. The kids stay off camera." "Focus broke, so we went outside." A parent who has had that day will feel it. He does not tell them to feel it.

### The skip-rate speech in the same Reel

**Flag. Not her point, and not the way this account is run.** One inverted number is not the business. What to watch is in the metrics section. TikTok being ahead of Instagram so far is a reason to read TikTok first. It is not a reason to worship one ratio.

## Pillars

Four. Faith is the first, and it also shows up in the other three, because the brand is not split. Three hooks each, in his voice. They are cards, not captions that have been approved. Most of them use common belief, then the honest contradiction.

### 1. The prayers this house prays

The Our Father, the Hail Mary, the Glory Be, the Jesus Prayer, and the rosary he leads. Saints and the season when the calendar is real. He does not add a devotion for the feed.

- "People think a Catholic homeschool day starts with a planner. We pray the Our Father, the Hail Mary, the Glory Be, and the Jesus Prayer."
- "A lot of these accounts show the kids praying. I lead the rosary. You will not see them."
- "I talk about a saint only when I can explain him in plain words to my own kids. Otherwise I leave him off the card."

### 2. The day we teach

No 8-to-3 table school. Outside when focus breaks. No certificate. He knows his kids. Prayer is part of the same day, not a separate brand.

- "People think homeschool means school from 8 to 3 at the table. We don't do that."
- "When focus is gone, we stop and play outside. The lesson can wait."
- "I don't have a teaching certificate. I'm their dad. I know them, and we still say the prayers we say."

### 3. The kids stay off camera

The rule that makes the faith and the school day believable. Holy pictures of children are still pictures of children.

- "You can know how we homeschool without seeing my children's faces."
- "If a post only works because my kids are in it, I don't make the post."
- "People think Catholic family content means the kids looking holy on camera. I talk about our faith. They stay off the screen."

### 4. A tool only when it does one job

AI and anything else. Screen recordings, made separately from the walk. The prayers are not a use case.

- "AI in this house is a screen recording I make later. The walk is just me talking."
- "People think a homeschool dad online is selling an app. I'm talking about our day. A tool gets in when it does one job."
- "I don't put an app between us and the prayers. The Our Father is not a prompt."

**Not pillars.** Curriculum wars. Liturgy wars. A certification series. Book ads. NetSuite. "Day in the life" with the kids blurred in the background. Anything that starts with "I'm excited to announce."

## Where a post goes, and which numbers to read

Film one walk. Put the same clip on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube Shorts. Do not film four versions. Do not cross-post it to LinkedIn.

**Cadence.** Two walks a week is enough to learn, and a working dad can keep it. One walk is enough in a heavy week. One true walk beats four he does not mean. The AI screen recording is occasional and separate. It is not the second walk by default.

**TikTok has outperformed Instagram so far.** That is his note. He did not say on which number. When he compares, he compares the same kind of number, average watch time against average watch time, on posts he actually published. Read TikTok first because that is where people have stayed with him. Keep posting the same clip to the other three. One strong TikTok does not mean the niche changes, and a quiet Instagram week does not mean he quits it.

| Place | Read these, if the app shows them | Leave alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | Average watch time. The share who watched the whole video. The point where people drop off. | A single "retention" score as the only number. It is not the inverse of an Instagram skip rate. TikTok's own analytics tutorial says no one engagement metric is the one that matters. |
| Instagram | Views, watch time, viewers, average watch time, and follows. Viewers is estimated and marked in development on Instagram's help page. | `reels_skip_rate`. That is a developer field for the first 3 seconds. It is not on the insight screen. |
| YouTube Shorts | "How many chose to view" versus swiped away. Watch time if the app shows it. | The name "swipe rate." That chip is not a curve of who stayed to the end. |
| Facebook | Views, average watch time, reach, and likes, comments, and shares, when insights are there. A retention graph only if that screen already has one. | A personal profile needs pro mode and at least 1,000 followers before Facebook shows Reel insights. Do not chase 1,000 followers to unlock a dashboard. If the screen is missing, skip the number. |

No new tool. No skip-rate tracker. No spreadsheet that pretends four apps share one score.
