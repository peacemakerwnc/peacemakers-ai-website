**What he actually shows**

Talking head over screen recordings of two Instagram carousels, with captions that repeat the voiceover.

Spoken method, condensed from the transcript:

1. Compare two carousels and guess which got more views. He says the winner is not the one you would pick.
2. The job of a carousel is to get the viewer to the last photo. He says Instagram treats that as high-quality content and pushes the post, and that this is "not a myth."
3. Give a reason to finish: number the hook. Examples he gives: "five things that you should do," "five things that I learned," "three things that I would never do again."
4. A number tells people how many swipes are left, so they continue.
5. He calls this the number-one hack for growing through carousels, says to repeat the format, and says an educational numbered list is better than a numbered list that is not educational.
6. The post people finish will beat the one that "looks like it's doing well" if viewers did not reach the end of that other post.

The "more views" example on screen is a numbered personal-life carousel from @semi_retired_athlete ("Here are 5 things I've done…"), not a business post. On-screen insight labels read as about 65.2K and 130K views. Those figures are OBSERVED from a compressed screen recording via OCR. They were not checked in Instagram insights.

Authoritative source checked: Adam Mosseri, "Instagram Ranking Explained," 31 May 2023, https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained. Creator reach notes on https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach point back to that post and do not add a carousel-completion signal.

What that post actually says for Feed: rank using activity (likes, shares, saves, comments), post popularity and speed of those actions, and who posted. The five interactions predicted most closely are spending a few seconds on the post, commenting, liking, sharing, and tapping the profile photo. Carousels are named only as a format people see in Feed. Reels are separate: predicted reshare, watch-through, like, and a visit to the audio page.

So extra swipes can matter only as a stand-in for "spend a few seconds," likes, saves, or shares. Instagram has not published "last slide reached" as a quality label or as the top carousel hack. His "this is real, not a myth" line overclaims a dwell tactic.
