**What he says**

Title card, the whole way through: "How to master content in 35 seconds (+178k followers)."

The notepad is a fill-in for one Reel, marked S to E. The words are tokens, not a sentence:

| Token | Role on the board |
| --- | --- |
| FAT / WALK | The topic and the obvious answer. Blue, then red. |
| BUT / WAY / ONLY IF | The turn. |
| A / B / C | Three candidates. The answer is held. |
| MOST / LEAST | A reversal. |

Spoken ladder, in order:

1. The flat line fails. "If you said the best way to lose fat is through walking, why would I keep watching? I'm no longer curious."
2. "Instead, use but only if." "The best way to lose fat is through walking, but only if you walk this specific way."
3. "But the stronger way is actually through elimination."
4. The withheld label. "I found the best way to lose fat. I thought it was A, but it wasn't. B, but it wasn't. It's actually C. Now they have to watch the entire Reel to get the payoff."
5. "But the strongest way is actually through contradiction." "The best way to lose fat is through walking, but the people who walk the most are seeing the least results. Now they're gonna stick around to see why."

"Stronger" and "strongest" are his ranking. He does not show a test. The fat-loss sentences are his illustration. This pass did not check them, and they are not a line to reuse.

Two comments read the tactic the way a buyer would. One says "So just lie." Another says "waste people's time." A third says the four shapes times four videos is sixteen videos. Those are reactions, not instructions.

**Claims**

| Claim | Label | What was checked |

| "+178k followers" under "master content in 35 seconds." | The count matches the account. The plus sign does not. | The Reel embed returned `edge_followed_by.count` 178,231 for `@iamchrischung` on this fetch. The card rounds that to +178k. The Reel itself had about 10,446 views and was posted the same day. A one-day Reel with that view count did not produce 178,000 followers. The number is the account total, worn as a result. |
| "Top 1% content." | UNVERIFIED | It is the heading on his notepad. The Reel defines no percentile and shows no distribution. |
| A flat claim loses the viewer. "But only if," elimination, a withheld A/B/C, and a most/least reversal keep them. | The mechanism is a known account of curiosity. The ranking is his. | Loewenstein, *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1), 1994, [CMU PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf), retrieved this pass. He describes curiosity as "cognitively induced deprivation that results from the perception of a gap in one's knowledge," and he lists a "tendency to disappoint when satisfied." He also notes that a wrong guess, corrected, makes the gap hard to ignore. That is the A/B/C move. It is not a retention study of this formula, and it is not a promise that a contractor account will land in a top percentile. The same paper says the gap disappoints people when the answer is thin. |
| "They have to watch the entire Reel to get the payoff." | OBSERVED as his instruction | Holding the answer is the tactic. It is also the move the "waste people's time" comment is about. For an owner who came to decide whether to hire someone, an empty wait is a cost. |
| People who walk the most see the least fat-loss results. | Not adopted | His example sentence. Not checked. Not a Peacemakers claim. |

**The part to leave alone.** The fat-loss script. The +178k badge. "Top 1%." A batch of sixteen videos from one sentence. Commenting on the Reel or DMing for a template. Any hook whose "but" is false, or whose answer never arrives.

**Effort.** This pass is the log. If James asks, three honest lines for his own Reels are a short writing job, using a case he has actually seen. No filming and no scheduler in that pass either.

**Risk.** A false contradiction in front of a $5–50M owner. Quoting 178k or top 1% as a Peacemakers result. An open loop that stalls and then delivers a thin answer. Treating a commenter's sixteen-video plan as a calendar.

**Compliance.** A business claim in a hook is still a claim. [15 U.S.C. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45) declares unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce unlawful. Retrieved this pass. The 2023 FTC blog URL in the practice playbook returned 404 on this pass, so the statute is the citation here. The playbook's rule still holds: marketing does not state a time saving, an error rate, or a dollar return unless that client's own test is attached. Follower counts and percentiles stay off the site and off proposals. A post, an email, or a filmed Reel is a material external communication and needs James (`HUMAN_APPROVAL_STANDARD.md`). No client file is involved.
