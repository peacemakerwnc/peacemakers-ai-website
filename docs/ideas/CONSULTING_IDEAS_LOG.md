## 2026-10-31 — Delete five things on a LinkedIn profile before writing new copy

**Status:** Logged only. James has not approved a profile edit.

**Classification:** (B) Peacemakers AI consulting. This is James's own marketing surface (the personal profile buyers open), with a narrow optional use inside a Blueprint when a client's buyers actually live on LinkedIn.

**Source:** [Instagram Reel Deye4JcunVl](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Deye4JcunVl/) by Nicole Marguerite Gray (`@nicolemargueritegray`), posted 2026-10-22. Slack note: "Read the description."

**What the Reel is:** 6.8 seconds. No speech. Background music only. On-screen text does not change: "five things I delete on my clients' LinkedIn AS SOON AS we start." The five edits are only in the caption. Comments sampled from the download are the word "CHECKLIST" (her lead magnet). That PDF was not retrieved.

### The method (from the caption)

Before writing new profile copy, remove:

1. Headline buzzwords (`passionate`, `results-driven`, `visionary`). Replace with who you work with, what you do for them, and one credibility line, in under 20 words.
2. Links painted on the banner. Use that image for proof and a simple call to action.
3. A stuffed Featured section. Keep three items, each with a custom thumbnail.
4. A filtered profile photo. Use a close-up, no filter, looking at the camera.
5. An About section written in the third person. Write as if the reader is already in your DMs.

She also says the profile then "instantly reads clearer" and that most clients finish in one evening. Those two results are her claim. They are not verified.

### Claim check (LinkedIn's own pages, fetched 2026-10-31)

| Caption claim | What LinkedIn actually documents |
| --- | --- |
| Headline should be under 20 words and free of buzzwords. | The platform field is 220 characters. LinkedIn's own talent blog says "You get 220 characters — so make them count" ([product tip](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/recruiters-with-eye-catching-linkedin-profile-headlines)). Help → [Edit Your Headline](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a542926) does not ban words like "passionate." Under 20 words is her editing rule inside the real limit. The same talent post tells readers they may talk about passions and mission. Specificity is the useful part. |
| Links in the banner cannot be clicked. | Confirmed for the cover image. [Add or change the cover image](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568217) is upload, crop, filters, and (on some paid plans) a slideshow. There is no URL field. Recommended size is 1584×396, JPG or PNG, under 8MB. LinkedIn has also removed the old Creator Mode website hyperlink ([Help a727760](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a727760)). The clickable control is a separate **custom button**, on Premium Business, Sales Navigator, and Recruiter, and it shows on the profile, posts, messages, and search. Deleting a real custom button because the Reel said "banner links" would remove the one link that works. |
| Featured should be three items max. | [Featured FAQs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a552452): unlimited items can be added; up to 400 display. Featured is on individual profiles, not company Pages. External links in Featured are real links. Logged-out viewers do not see Featured. Three items is a presentation choice so the row stays a proof shelf. |
| Filtered photos should be deleted; use a close-up looking at the camera. | [Profile photo guidelines](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1377087): the photo must reflect your likeness. LinkedIn may remove a photo that is only a logo, emoji, landscape, animal, words, flag, baby photo, or someone else's likeness (including stock). The photo editor itself includes filters ([add or change photo](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541850)). A heavy filter is a trust problem when selling to owners, and a likeness problem if it stops looking like James. It is not a listed ban. [Create a good LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351) says members with a profile photo receive up to 2× more profile views. That multiplier is LinkedIn's claim. |
| About must not be third person. | The same help page says the About section should express mission, motivation, and skills in one or two paragraphs (bullets are fine). It does not require first person and it does not forbid third person. First person aimed at the owner is the right voice for this firm. It is a writing choice. |
