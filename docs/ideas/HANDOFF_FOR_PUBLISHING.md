**Classification:** (A) @buildingpeacemakers. James's note on the drop was "apply this to homeschool." That overrides the fact that the source reel is a content-production tutorial.

**Source:** https://www.instagram.com/reel/De4hgvzFIAl/ — `prestonchinbuilds` (Preston Chin). James dropped it 26 October 2026, 8:03 PM ET. About 67 seconds.

**What the source actually is:** He films a walk-and-talk. Before he leaves he writes nine hooks (three pains, three outcomes, three curiosities) and one body with three points. He records the hooks one after another and then the body. If he stumbles he keeps walking. Afterward he drops the footage into Claude, set to a high-effort model, and tells it to cut mistakes and dead air and to put each hook in front of the same body. The caption says comment `B154X` for his guide. A card in the reel is labeled "Simulated demo."

**Do not carry over:** his comment keyword, his claim that one walk becomes nine finished videos, the view counts he puts on other creators' examples, or their scripts. Do not upload a family walk, a child's voice, or a school day to a consumer Claude account. Do not use his footage.

**Hook in the first 2 seconds:** The first words and the first on-screen line are the pain or the curiosity, not the setup. Example, spoken and on screen together: "Homeschool morning started with a fight."

**Structure:**

1. Hook (one sentence, the pain or the odd fact).
2. Buy-in (one sentence on why this Tuesday matters).
3. Three short points, each opened with a line that makes you want the next one.
4. A plain takeaway a mother can do tomorrow.
5. No hard sell. If a book or a printable exists and fits, one true sentence at the end. If it does not fit, leave it out.

**Pacing:** Keep moving or keep the hands in frame. Do not stop the recording for a stumble. Cut the stumble later, by hand, unless James has approved a tool for that edit. One body can wear two or three hooks. Nine is the source reel's number, not a quota.

**On-screen text:** Short, navy on cream, sage as the accent. Words to include, in the text and not only in the caption: homeschool, faith at home, and the specific practice (read-aloud, morning, memory work). No stock footage of children.

**Audio:** James's voice, or a quiet bed he has the rights to. The audio title repeats the niche words, for example "Homeschool morning, faith at home."

**Format:** Reel, about 30 to 45 seconds, vertical. James on camera, or hands and books. No child's face. A carousel is the alternate if he would rather not walk: one hook shape per slide, same colors, same words.

**Three ideas (drafts only):**

1. **Reel. One walk, three hooks, one morning.** Body: the order of a faith-at-home morning (Scripture, a read-aloud, one lesson) and what he stopped doing so the morning would fit. Hooks to record on that same walk, each as its own first two seconds:
   - Pain: "Homeschool morning started with a fight."
   - Outcome: "The main lesson is done before lunch."
   - Curiosity: "The one thing we took off the morning list."
   First caption line: "Homeschool morning, faith at home: one walk, three ways to start."
   Audio title: "Homeschool morning, faith at home."

2. **Carousel. Three hook shapes, no borrowed scripts.** Five slides, cream ground, navy type, sage rule.
   - Slide 1: "Homeschool, faith at home. Three ways to open a reel."
   - Slide 2: "I have done this morning a long time. Here are three things I no longer do." (credential, then a real list)
   - Slide 3: "Three cheap ways to make read-aloud actually happen." (a number, then the list)
   - Slide 4: "People say start earlier. Here is the step that mattered." (the how, in one step)
   - Slide 5: one true next thing, or the book title only if that book is the subject.
   First caption line: "Homeschool read-aloud, faith at home: three ways to open."
   Audio title, if the carousel has a voice: "Homeschool read-aloud, faith at home."

3. **Reel. Keep rolling when the explanation stumbles.** James tells one read-aloud point, messes a sentence, keeps going, and the cut removes the mess. On-screen text at the start: "Homeschool read-aloud, said plainly." The point of the reel is the read-aloud, not a lesson about filming. First caption line: "Homeschool read-aloud, faith at home, in one take." Audio title: "Homeschool read-aloud, faith at home."
