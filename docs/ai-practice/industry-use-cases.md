# Industry use cases

**Peacemakers AI · internal**
**Research date:** 17 October 2026

These are workflows a $5–50 million company in North Carolina already does by hand. They are not a pipeline and they are not a claim that any named prospect has the problem. Charter Building Group is the reference client for the builder pattern (a designer's handwritten selections become a branded packet, and an architectural-review form gets filled). Charter is not a row in the prospect screen.

The Koerner Office episode 344, in an unverified local transcript, describes a different firm's assessment: interviews, redacted real documents, and a live demo in which a storm-structure inspection became a price proposal. The transcript's "about an hour," "about five minutes," and "$3,575 versus $3,375" are that episode's story. They are not Peacemakers measurements. Nothing below uses those figures. Every time estimate is an **assumption for a first conversation**, to be replaced by what the client's own people say in the interviews.

Two build levels:

- **Blueprint quick win.** One document type, a rate card or a form, a person reviews, and you can show it in an assessment from redacted samples.
- **Monthly AI department.** It touches a system of record, several roles, or a permission boundary. It belongs on a retainer, after the quick win has earned the trust.

Data sensitivity follows the playbook: low, moderate, high. High does not go in a model.

The best assessment demo in each industry is marked **Show this first.** Two or three per industry are marked **Assessment demo.**

The working demo in [demo/](demo/) is the show-this-first pattern for commercial maintenance, HVAC service, and builder selections, using fictional companies.

## Home builders

Custom and semi-custom builders. The paperwork is selections, allowances, estimates, and the forms a community makes them file. Charter is the reference shape. The prospect screen's first wave (Hagood, Dickerson, Living Stone, Paragon, and others) is the same kind of work. Do not quote those firms' directory revenues.

### Show this first · Selections worksheet to a client packet and a review form

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A designer writes flooring, cabinets, colors, and upgrades on a worksheet or in a notebook. Someone retypes that into a client PDF and again into the community's architectural review form. Quantities get mistyped. Allowances and upgrades get mixed.
- **With help.** The worksheet is read into line items. Included items stay at zero. Upgrades price from the builder's allowance table, which the builder can edit. A person checks quantities. The same fields fill the client packet and the review form.
- **Inputs.** The worksheet, the allowance table, the plan name, the lot. Buyer phone and email stay out of the model.
- **Outputs.** A branded selections packet and a filled review form, both drafts.
- **Human review.** The designer, before either PDF leaves the office. The buyer does not see a draft the designer has not signed.
- **Time assumption.** On the order of an hour of retyping and checking, against a few minutes of review, for one lot. Replace this with the designer's own number.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate. Names and contact details. Not a credit file.
- **Why it demos.** You can do it from one redacted worksheet in the room. The fictional version is Harborline Homes in the demo.

### Allowance drift across a house

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** The estimate, the selections, and the change orders each have their own allowance balance. The superintendent finds the mismatch at invoice time.
- **With help.** The three documents are lined up on allowance codes. Differences are listed. Nothing posts to the job cost system.
- **Inputs.** Estimate, signed selections, change orders. Dollars and codes, not the buyer's financing.
- **Outputs.** A one-page exception list.
- **Human review.** The estimator, before anyone talks to the buyer.
- **Time assumption.** A long afternoon of cross-checking a house, against a first pass the estimator can finish in a sitting. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate. Prices are confidential. They are not personal information under the North Carolina breach definition, and they are still not for a personal chatbot.

### Vendor bid leveled against the estimate

**Blueprint quick win, if the bids are already in a folder.**

- **Today.** Three subcontractors send bids in three formats. The estimator rebuilds them on a spreadsheet to see who is light on a scope.
- **With help.** Each bid is read into the estimator's scope rows. Missing rows are flagged. The low number is not declared the winner.
- **Inputs.** The bids and the scope list.
- **Outputs.** A comparison grid with gaps marked.
- **Human review.** The estimator. Awarding the work stays a person, because a missing exclusion is how a bid looks cheap.
- **Time assumption.** A couple of hours per trade package, against a first grid in well under an hour. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate. Vendor pricing.

### Spec section to a submittal cover

**Monthly AI department** once it is tied to the project folder and a naming standard.

- **Today.** A project engineer copies spec sections onto submittal covers and transmittals.
- **With help.** The spec section number, the product, and the required action are drafted onto the cover. The file is not filed by itself.
- **Inputs.** The spec section the client is willing to share.
- **Outputs.** A cover draft.
- **Human review.** The project engineer, every time. A wrong spec section is a rejected submittal.
- **Time assumption.** Minutes per cover either way. The value is consistency, not a dramatic hour count. Do not sell this as the hour-saver.
- **Sensitivity.** Low to moderate. No homeowner data if you keep it to the spec.

### Schedule slip written up for the buyer

**Not an assessment demo.** Monthly, and only after the selections packet is trusted.

- **Today.** The superintendent knows why a house slipped and writes a stiff email at 9 p.m.
- **With help.** Notes become a draft the superintendent edits. The send button is the superintendent's.
- **Inputs.** The superintendent's notes. Not the buyer's financing, not a complaint file.
- **Outputs.** An email draft.
- **Human review.** The superintendent sends it. Always.
- **Time assumption.** The writing is short. The judgment is the whole job. Do not quote a large time save.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

## Commercial and general contractors

Maintenance, small commercial, and the storm-structure pattern from the Koerner episode: an inspection in, a price out, a person between them.

### Show this first · Inspection report to a maintenance proposal

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A crew inspects structures, writes debris and access on a report, and someone in the office prices a proposal an hour later from memory and a rate sheet. The proposal goes to an HOA or a property manager.
- **With help.** The report's structure list is read. Debris class drives the rate card. Mobilization and disposal are lines the contractor can edit. The total is arithmetic.
- **Inputs.** The inspection. Customer phone, email, and account number stay in a local block.
- **Outputs.** A draft proposal PDF.
- **Human review.** The estimator, before it is sent. The demo will not export until someone checks the box and signs initials.
- **Time assumption.** Interviews in the episode described about an hour by hand. Use the client's number, not the episode's. A first assumption for a conversation is "most of an hour, down to a short review," and it stays an assumption until they time five jobs.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.
- **Why it demos.** The prospect recognizes the page. The fictional version is Cape Fear Drainage Co. in the demo. Do not use a real client's unredacted report on a screen you do not control.

### Daily report to a change-order draft

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** The superintendent's daily report mentions extra work. The change order is written days later, after the detail has faded.
- **With help.** Extra-work sentences become a draft change order with quantities left blank where the note is vague.
- **Inputs.** The daily report. No wage rates, no medical notes.
- **Outputs.** A draft change order with holes where the note was thin.
- **Human review.** The project manager prices it and decides whether it is a change or the original scope.
- **Time assumption.** Writing the draft is the short part. Getting the facts is the long part. Say that out loud so you do not oversell it.
- **Sensitivity.** Low to moderate.

### Pay application checked against the schedule of values

**Monthly AI department.** It touches money.

- **Today.** A project accountant ties this period's pay app to the schedule of values and the stored materials.
- **With help.** Mismatches are listed. The pay app is not submitted.
- **Inputs.** The schedule of values and the draft pay app. Bank details never.
- **Outputs.** An exception list.
- **Human review.** The accountant. Submission stays in the accounting system, by a person.
- **Time assumption.** Half a day on a messy job, less on a clean one. Assumption only. Do not average it.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate to high if the packet includes lien waivers with tax identifiers. Strip those first.

### RFI log cleaned up

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** RFIs live in email. The log is a week behind.
- **With help.** A folder of sent RFIs is listed with the question, the spec section if it is named, and whether an answer is in the thread.
- **Inputs.** The RFI emails, with addresses redacted if the content is what you need.
- **Outputs.** A log the PM can edit.
- **Human review.** The PM, before the log is treated as official.
- **Time assumption.** An afternoon of inbox archaeology, against a first list. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### Safety walk notes to a punchlist

**Blueprint quick win only if names and injuries are removed first.**

- **Today.** A walk produces a photo roll and a notebook. The punchlist is typed later.
- **With help.** Observations become a list of locations and fixes. Anything that looks like an injury or a name is left out of the model and handled on the OSHA side by a person.
- **Inputs.** Notes with names and medical details already removed.
- **Outputs.** A punchlist draft.
- **Human review.** The safety lead.
- **Time assumption.** Modest. The point is that the list exists the same day.
- **Sensitivity.** High if you skip the removal step. Moderate after it.

## Home services

HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and the companies that live on service tickets plus project work. T.A. Woods is the kind of Wilmington mechanical firm on the prospect screen. The demo company is not them.

### Show this first · Tech notes to a service quote

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** The tech writes what failed, tells the homeowner a round number, and the office retypes a quote that may not match the notes.
- **With help.** The notes become parts and labor lines. The company's flat-rate table sets the dollars. A rule such as "diagnostic trip drops off if the repair is approved" is in the table, not in the model's head.
- **Inputs.** The tech notes. The homeowner's phone and address stay local.
- **Outputs.** A quote PDF.
- **Human review.** The dispatcher or the owner, before it is texted to the homeowner. The tech does not need a new app on day one. Email the notes in.
- **Time assumption.** Half an hour of office retyping and callbacks, against a few minutes of review. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate. No card numbers. If the tech photographed a card, that photo does not enter the workflow.
- **Why it demos.** Oak Island Comfort Co. in the demo. One ticket, one rate card, a visible gap between the truck number and the worksheet.

### Maintenance-agreement renewal list

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** Someone exports last year's agreements and hand-builds a call list of who is due.
- **With help.** The export is grouped into "due this month," "lapsed," and "missing a date." No one is emailed automatically.
- **Inputs.** A spreadsheet of agreement dates and equipment. Not card data.
- **Outputs.** A call list.
- **Human review.** The person who makes the calls. They decide the words.
- **Time assumption.** A morning of spreadsheet work once a month. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### Callback classified against the original ticket

**Monthly AI department**, because it needs the ticket history.

- **Today.** A callback comes in and a dispatcher hunts the prior ticket to see if it is our part, our labor, or a new failure.
- **With help.** The new complaint and the prior ticket are summarized side by side with a suggested class. Warranty dollars are not issued.
- **Inputs.** Both tickets, redacted.
- **Outputs.** A classification for the dispatcher to accept or change.
- **Human review.** The dispatcher. Warranty is money.
- **Time assumption.** Ten to twenty minutes of hunting per callback. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### Install proposal from a load calc and a site note

**Blueprint quick win** when the price book is already a table.

- **Today.** A comfort advisor rebuilds a proposal from a load calculation, a site sketch, and a price book.
- **With help.** Equipment options are drafted from the price book. The advisor changes them.
- **Inputs.** The calc, the note, the price book.
- **Outputs.** A draft proposal with two or three options, not a single "best."
- **Human review.** The advisor. They are the one who stood in the house.
- **Time assumption.** An hour of assembly. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### Inventory exception from truck stock counts

**Monthly**, and only after the quote is trusted. Low drama.

- **Today.** Truck counts are texted in. Someone updates a sheet.
- **With help.** The texts become a variance list against par.
- **Inputs.** Counts. No customer data.
- **Outputs.** A reorder list.
- **Human review.** The warehouse lead. Ordering stays in their system.
- **Time assumption.** Small. Do not lead the assessment with it.
- **Sensitivity.** Low.

**Monthly AI department** if the report is a compliance document the AHJ reads.

- **Today.** A tech dumps meter or flow readings into a form the inspector expects.

### Change-order from a marked-up drawing note

**Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A sketch note in the margin becomes a change order two weeks later.
- **With help.** The note becomes a draft with the quantity blank if the note has no count.
- **Inputs.** The note. Not the whole drawing set, unless they want to pay for that.
- **Outputs.** A draft.
- **Human review.** The PM.

- **Sensitivity.** Low.

## Light manufacturers

Often on NetSuite, which is familiar ground. The win is not "replace NetSuite." The win is getting a clean draft into the transaction someone already posts.

### Show this first · Customer spec email to a quote line sheet

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A sales engineer reads an email and a sketch note and builds a configured quote in a spreadsheet, then retypes it into NetSuite.
- **With help.** The email becomes a line sheet in the company's item codes, with blanks where the spec is silent. NetSuite is not written to.
- **Inputs.** The email and the item master extract. No ACH details.

- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.
- **Why it demos.** You can show the line sheet without a NetSuite login. Connecting the import is the retainer, and only after their administrator wants it.

## Property managers

The prospect screen includes firms such as Henderson Properties and Rhyne Management as the kind of local manager in view. Work is notices, owner packets, and vendor invoices. Tenant screening is a different, higher-risk product. Do not demo it.

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **With help.** The notes become a work order with vendor type and a blank for price. The owner's approval is still required before work starts.
- **Inputs.** The notes. Resident medical details, if any, stay out. A service-animal note is not a repair item.
- **Outputs.** A draft work order.
- **Human review.** The manager, then the owner if the management agreement says so.
- **Time assumption.** Twenty to forty minutes of writing per inspection. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### Vendor invoice checked against the approval

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** An invoice arrives for more than the owner approved. Someone notices, or doesn't.

- **Time assumption.** A few minutes per invoice, which is the point at volume. Do not multiply by a guessed invoice count.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate. High if the invoice image shows a bank account for ACH. Crop that off before the model sees it.

- **Inputs.** The lease. Social Security numbers and bank drafts removed first.
- **Outputs.** An abstract.
- **Human review.** The manager, against the lease, before a notice goes out.
- **Time assumption.** Half an hour per lease. Assumption only.

## Insurance agencies and title companies

Read the playbook's insurance and title sections before you offer any of these. Bulletin 24-B-19 speaks to licensed insurers. An agency is not the insurer. A tool that prices or declines coverage is not an assessment demo. Wire instructions never enter a model.

### Show this first · Submission email to an underwriter summary

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win. Agency, not carrier pricing.**

- **Today.** A producer reads a submission, ACORD forms, and a loss run, then writes the underwriter a summary.
- **With help.** Named insured, locations, limits requested, and the years on the loss run are listed. A recommended price is not produced.
- **Inputs.** The submission, with account numbers, Social Security numbers, and driver's license numbers removed first.
- **Outputs.** A summary memo.
- **Human review.** The producer, before it goes to a market. They are responsible for what the market is told.
- **Time assumption.** Half an hour to an hour per new business submission. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** High until identifiers are removed. This is the workflow where redaction has to be visible in the demo, the way the assessment demo shows the redacted payload.
- **Why it demos.** The prospect sees their own memo shape. You do not show a premium.

### Policy-change request checked against the dec page

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A CSR reads a client's email ("add a truck") against the dec page and types an endorsement request.
- **With help.** The email and the dec page produce a checklist of what is being asked and what the dec page currently shows. The endorsement is not issued.
- **Inputs.** The email and the dec page. Strip account numbers.
- **Outputs.** A checklist.
- **Human review.** The CSR. Issuing the endorsement stays in the agency system.
- **Time assumption.** Fifteen to thirty minutes. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** High until stripped, then moderate.

### Loss-run trend for a renewal meeting

**Monthly**, and only with the carrier's and the client's permission to use the runs.

- **Today.** A producer highlights three years of runs with a pen.
- **With help.** Counts by year and cause are tabulated. Causes are the words on the run, not a new coding scheme the model invents.
- **Inputs.** The runs, identifiers removed.
- **Outputs.** A count table.
- **Human review.** The producer.
- **Time assumption.** An hour at renewal for a larger account. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** High.

### Title · commitment exceptions listed for a processor

**Assessment demo only with a fictional commitment, or a real one the underwriter has redacted in writing.**

- **Today.** A processor reads a commitment and lists the exceptions that need a document or a payoff.
- **With help.** Exceptions are listed and grouped. Requirements are not marked satisfied.
- **Inputs.** The commitment. Wiring instructions removed even if someone pasted them on the scan. Names of parties can stay if the underwriter says so, and they stay out of a consumer chatbot either way.
- **Outputs.** A checklist.
- **Human review.** The processor, against the commitment. This is not a title opinion.
- **Time assumption.** Half an hour on a thick commitment. Assumption only.
- **Sensitivity.** High.
- **Why it is not the first thing you show a builder.** It is the first thing you show a title prospect, and only after you have said, out loud, that wire changes are out of scope.

### Title · wire-instruction change

**Do not build this.** The existing callback procedure is the control. A model that drafts a new wire instruction is a worse process, not a better one.

## Multi-location franchises

The pattern is the same report from many sites, written by people who do not all write the same way. A franchisee's customer list is still the franchisee's data. Do not pool personal information across franchisees unless the franchise agreement and the owners both say so.

### Show this first · Store close notes to a weekly ops recap

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** Each location texts or emails a close. Someone at the group rewrites it into a weekly note for the owner.
- **With help.** The closes become one recap: sales the POS already calculated, labor hours they already counted, and the exceptions in the notes. The model does not compute sales.
- **Inputs.** The close notes and the POS totals. No employee medical notes, no applicant data.
- **Outputs.** A one-page recap.
- **Human review.** The area manager, before the owner sees it.
- **Time assumption.** A few hours on Monday. Assumption only. Ask how long Monday actually takes.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.
- **Why it demos.** You can mock three fictional stores on one page. It feels like their week.

### Guest complaint to a response draft

**Assessment demo. Blueprint quick win.**

- **Today.** A manager writes a careful reply, late.
- **With help.** The complaint becomes a draft in the brand's tone, with a blank where a comp or a refund would be. The refund is a person.
- **Inputs.** The complaint. No card number, even if the guest typed one.
- **Outputs.** A draft reply.
- **Human review.** The manager sends it.
- **Time assumption.** Ten minutes of staring. The save is consistency across locations, which you should not convert into a dollar claim without their numbers.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

### New-hire packet checklist

**Not an assessment demo if it scores people.** A checklist of missing forms is fine. Ranking applicants is the NYC / Title VII problem in the playbook, and it is out.

- **Today.** A manager hunts for the I-9, the uniform size, and the schedule acknowledgment.
- **With help.** A list of what is present and what is missing. No scoring.
- **Inputs.** Form names, not the contents of identity documents.
- **Outputs.** A checklist.
- **Human review.** The manager.
- **Time assumption.** Small per hire. Real if they hire constantly. Get the hire count from them.
- **Sensitivity.** High if identity documents are in the folder. The workflow should never open those images.

### Local marketing calendar from what each store already did

**Monthly.**

- **Today.** Each store posts when it remembers. The brand finds out later.
- **With help.** A month of "what we posted / what we did not" from the stores' own notes.
- **Inputs.** The notes. Not a purchased consumer list.
- **Outputs.** A gap list.
- **Human review.** The marketer.
- **Time assumption.** Modest. Do not lead with it.
- **Sensitivity.** Low.

### Royalty or fee packet checked against the reported sales

**Monthly AI department.** Money, and the franchisor's audit rights.

- **Today.** Reported sales, the POS export, and the fee worksheet are checked by hand.
- **With help.** Differences are listed. The fee is not submitted.
- **Inputs.** The totals. Not employee pay detail.
- **Outputs.** An exception list.
- **Human review.** The accountant.
- **Time assumption.** Ask the accountant. This is their control, and they may not want it sped up. Believe that too.
- **Sensitivity.** Moderate.

## What to take into a first assessment

Pick one **Show this first** workflow the owner actually recognized on the call. Ask them to send two redacted examples and the rate sheet or form they already use. Build the demo from those, the way the episode described, with the safety steps in the playbook visible: the contact block held back, the redacted text on screen, a rate card they can edit, a person approving the PDF, and no claim that the minutes are anything but their own.
