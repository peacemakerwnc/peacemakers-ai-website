## Claims, and whether they hold

### Valid

**A written skill folder is a real way to reuse a procedure.** His site shows a `content-engine` tree (`AGENTS.md`, skill folders, `pipeline.md`, `qa.md`). That is ordinary documentation an agent can read. It does not, by itself, publish anything or earn anything.

**Official APIs can publish to accounts you own, inside platform limits.** Meta’s Instagram content-publishing guide says professional accounts publish through the Instagram API with the content-publish permission, and that accounts “are limited to 100 API-published posts within a 24-hour moving period.” The same guide’s carousel section also says “50 published posts within a 24-hour period.” Design to the stricter number. The guide also provides `is_ai_generated=true` so a post can disclose AI. Posterract’s terms say it depends on those platform APIs and that Meta may reject a post. TikTok requires a creator label on realistic AI-generated image, audio, and video. YouTube requires creators to disclose GenAI that meaningfully alters or generates photorealistic content, and does not require disclosure for non-realistic content or minor aesthetic edits.

**Draft, then a person approves, is a sound pattern.** Posterract’s own terms require review before publish. That matches how Peacemakers already treats external publishing: a person approves it. The sample “invoice agent” on AI for Savages is explicitly a preview on `ledgerly.example.com` (“drafts the invoices and texts him to approve”). The shape is fine. The card is not evidence that the agent works, and the “$6 VPS” line is part of that sample, not a measured bill.

**KDP and Etsy already allow disclosed, human-directed creative work.** KDP requires disclosure when an AI tool creates the text, images, translations, cover, or interior, even if you edit afterward. KDP does not require disclosure when you wrote or drew the work and only used AI to edit, check, or brainstorm. Etsy allows “seller-prompted AI creations” under “Designed by a seller,” and requires the listing description to say AI was used. Etsy does not allow AI prompt bundles.

### Questionable

**“Stop prompting. Build agent loops.”** Caption only, TikTok 21 Jun 2026 UTC: “STOP PROMPTING AI AGENTS. Start Building AI Agent LOOPS.” A loop that drafts and waits is reasonable. A loop that posts, spends, or emails without a stop condition is not. The caption does not show which one he means.

**Local models versus a VPS, as a blanket rule.** Inside the same window he posted “STOP USING LOCAL AI MODELS” (14 Jul 2026 UTC), “the TRUTH about KNOWLEDGE GRAPHS” (12 Jul 2026 UTC), “AI Agent + SQLite + knowledge graph … VPS” (20 Jul 2026 UTC), and “the OPEN-SOURCE tech stack to deploy to your VPS” (25 Aug 2026 UTC; a third-party page displayed 24 Aug). Those captions point in opposite directions. Which one saves money depends on the workload, and none of the videos were transcribed.

**“You approve, or you don't have to.”** The marketing line is looser than Posterract’s terms, which put review on the customer and forbid spam and coordinated inauthentic behavior. Connecting “up to 100 accounts on each platform” is the kind of setup those terms tell the customer not to abuse. Unattended posting also skips Instagram’s AI flag, TikTok’s realistic-AI label, and YouTube’s photorealistic disclosure unless someone sets them.

**News-reaction captions.** “Sam Altman will DESTROY Apple” (30 Apr 2026 UTC), “ChatGPT Codex can spawn Claude subagents” (31 May 2026 UTC), “How Odysseus by PewDiePie ACTUALLY WORKS” (3 Jun 2026 UTC), and “Kimi K3 puts China AHEAD in the AI race” (17 Jul 2026 UTC). **$0 MRR next to a paid membership.** The personal site displays $0 MRR on the same day the membership page offers $59.99/month. Either figure could be stale, a joke, or true. Neither is verified revenue. Do not treat the membership as proof of income.

## What to implement

Four changes. Each one uses a practice that checked out, aimed at a business you already run. Payoff is described as the work it removes. No dollar return is claimed, because none was measured.

### 1. @buildingpeacemakers — one teaching, one approved pack

**Why.** The skill-folder idea is valid. The virality promise is not. You already post the same homeschool teaching to Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. A short written procedure can turn one recording into a caption, a still, and a short, and stop there until you approve it.

**Effort.** Low. A one-page checklist. No new subscription.

**Payoff.** Less reformatting on posts you were going to make anyway. Not a growth guarantee.

**First step.** On the next short you already plan to publish, write the caption yourself, note whether any edit is realistic AI, and set the platform label only if it is: TikTok’s creator label for realistic AI, YouTube’s “AI use” setting for photorealistic GenAI, Instagram’s `is_ai_generated` if you ever publish through the official API. Non-realistic or minor edits do not need the YouTube disclosure.

### 2. Peacemakers Publishing — assisted versus generated, before upload

**Why.** KDP and Etsy already draw this line. Using a model to create children’s-book text or art and listing it as if you made it by hand conflicts with both policies. Using a model to edit or brainstorm work you actually made does not require a KDP disclosure. Etsy still wants AI disclosed in the listing when the image or design was generated from your prompt.

**Effort.** Low. A yes/no on each file before the next upload.

**Payoff.** Fewer listing problems and an honest product page. No sales forecast.

**First step.** For the next Amazon title and the next Etsy printable, mark each asset “I made this; AI only edited or brainstormed” or “a model created this.” Disclose the second kind on KDP, and in the Etsy description. Do not sell prompt bundles.

### 3. Peacemakers AI — drafts only, into the publisher you already have

**Why.** The blog cluster already publishes on a schedule from articles in this repo. A model can outline or rough-draft the next article, or a Blueprint prep note, and stop. You edit. The existing publisher ships the article. That is the valid half of his agent-loop caption, and it does not require Posterract, the $59.99 membership, or a new scheduler.

**Effort.** Low if the first use is one outline you paste and edit. Medium only if you later wire that draft into the repo. Do not start with the wiring.

**Payoff.** A faster first draft of work the Monday publisher is already going to need. Quality still depends on your edit.

**First step.** Take the next unpublished small-business article title and have a model produce an outline only. Edit it in the repo the way you edit the others. Do not let a model publish, email a client, or post to social.

### 4. Leave Charter Building Group on the evidence path you already use

**Why.** The invoice-draft sample on his site is the right shape for a shop that wants a draft and a human yes. It is also fake sample data. Charter does not get a copy of that demo because a creator showed a mock ledger.

**Effort.** None until a real Charter workflow is in front of you with its own evidence.

**Payoff.** Avoids building a second product from a screenshot.

**First step.** If Charter asks for this kind of draft-and-approve step, run it through the Blueprint process you already use. Do not buy his skill pack as the design.
