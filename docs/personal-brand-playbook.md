## Her points

Each block is her point in one line, whether it holds, and what James does with it.

### 1. Stop posting content and start building a common enemy

**Holds as a method, not as a result.** Naming a problem people already have is useful. "A common enemy builds the brand" was not measured on the Reel. A commenter on the Reel said the frame only divides. Donald Miller's *Building a StoryBrand* workbook, which another commenter named, asks for the root cause of the customer's problem and warns that too much fear makes people leave. Use a little. Do not found a cult.

**For James.** The problem finance teams already have: the system went live, and the close still depends on workarounds and one person who knows where the numbers live. Say that. Do not name an enemy class (implementers, Oracle, "agencies," Cirrus, a trade). The other Cirrus team does implementations. His public posts do not score their work.

### 2. People do not want more content. They want a culture, a community, and a movement

**Half holds.** People stay for a person they recognize and a problem they share. James does not need a movement, a membership, or cult-like fans. He needs finance people who trust his judgment, and parents who recognize a dad telling the truth. Small and specific beats a crowd he has to perform for.

**For James.** Two small rooms, not one movement. LinkedIn: finance teams after go-live. `@buildingpeacemakers`: faith and the homeschool, kids off camera. He already has the format — a card, a walk, unscripted. That is the culture. He does not announce one.

### 3. Give them an enemy to band against and a hero to root for

**The hero half holds. The war half does not.** The person with the problem is the hero. James is the one who has done the unglamorous part and will say what he saw.

**For James.** Hero on LinkedIn: the controller or the finance manager who still has to close. Hero on the family account: the parent doing the day. He does not ask anyone to root for him, his company, or his employer.

### 4. Name what you stand against — hustle culture, the nine-to-five trap, the devil

**The instruction holds. Her three examples do not.** "Name what you stand against" is clear. The examples are hers, from a creator business. They are a bad fit and, in one case, a conflict with his job.

**For James.** What he stands against, in a sentence he can say on a walk: "Go-live is not the finish." Beside it: a new AI tool is not the first fix, and a family is not content. Strike hustle culture, the nine-to-five, and "the devil" as brand lines. Faith stays faith. It is not borrowed as an enemy to grow an account.

### 5. This creates cult-like raving fans who rally to the mission

**Drop.** "Cult-like" is her claim. Nothing on the Reel shows those fans, and the word is the wrong aim. Peacemakers' own standard is the opposite: human judgment, no inflated claims, no pressure. A Catholic dad selling "cult-like" anything would be off, even as a joke.

**For James.** He wants to be useful and believed. A finance lead who saves a post, or a parent who finishes a walk-and-talk, is the win. No rally, no inside language, no "if you ever want to be rich."

### 6. Drop your niche

**Holds for the person. Does not hold for the firm.** The man in the cap agrees with her on personal brands and then draws the line this practice should keep: a company that sells services stays tightly niched. The Gary Vaynerchuk exchange he plays (start wide after Wine Library, because the content finds the audience) was not found as a primary interview. That clip stays unknown outside this Reel. Do not cite Gary. Do not "go wide" on the company because an algorithm might sort it out.

**For James.** He already has more than one life. Forcing them into one niche makes a mush, and posting all of them on LinkedIn makes a man who looks unfocused while he is employed. The fix is rooms, not a single wider niche. LinkedIn stays on after go-live. The family account stays on Catholic homeschool, faith and the day together. Peacemakers AI stays on its buyer and its ladder (the $3,500 Blueprint, then a monthly tier). "Drop your niche" does not change the offer or the price.

### 7. Build pillars around you: values, beliefs, skills, and controversial takes

**Holds, if the takes are ones he already believes.** Pillars are how a person is recognizable without pretending to be one topic. Manufactured controversy is a costume. His real disagreements are already plain.

**For James.** The pillars are in the next section. The takes he is willing to repeat:

- Go-live is the start of the work, not the trophy.
- Most teams need the system they already pay for to be trusted before they need another AI tool.
- A services company stays niched. A person can have more than one room. The rooms do not share a bio.
- The kids do not go on the internet to grow an account.
- He does not post income, and he does not tell anyone this path will make them rich.

He does not swear for effect, pick fights with a trade, or perform a hot take he would not say to a client.

### 8. What you are selling, they buy you

**Partly holds.** Heard in the Reel just before tip three. People do stay because of the person. Finance teams still hire a firm. Cirrus is the firm on the NetSuite work. Peacemakers AI is the firm on the small-business AI work. "They buy you" is a dangerous slogan while he is on someone else's payroll: it slides into freelancing against his employer, or into a guru pose.

**For James.** Be specific and easy to trust. On LinkedIn he is the person who stays after go-live and helps the finance team run what they bought. On the family account he is a dad telling the truth on a walk. He does not say "they buy you." He says what he does.

### 9. Forget looking polished

**Holds.** He already films this way: unscripted, from a card, outside. Waiting for a studio, a new site, or a script is how a post never happens.

**For James.** A phone walk is enough. One take is enough if it is true. Cut only what is wrong or what shows a child. Do not dress the family account up to look like a studio brand. Do not dress LinkedIn up like a Reel with captions shouting. A short written post in the same voice is fine when he is not walking.

### 10. Unpolished videos "made me 100k in a single month"

**Drop. Unproven.** This pass heard "and maybe a hundred K in a single month." Token probabilities on that span: "maybe" 0.82, "a" 0.33, "hundred" 0.72, "K" 0.84. The consulting log's other pass heard "made me 100k," with "100k" at 0.66. The words are not stable. The phone clip in the Reel says "If you ever want to be rich you NEED to know this." None of that is an audit. None of it is a Peacemakers number, a Cirrus number, or a promise.

**For James.** He does not repeat the figure, the "be rich" card, or any cousin of it ("six figures," "I made," a screenshot of a Stripe balance). The practice rule already says he does not claim a time saving, an error rate, or a dollar return unless that client's own test is attached. Personal posts follow the same rule. A certification and a job title are facts. A revenue line from her video is not.

### 11. People connect with real, not with perfect

**Holds.** This is the part of tip three worth keeping after the income line is thrown out.

**For James.** Real means a true detail: a close that still lives in a spreadsheet next to NetSuite, a card in his pocket, a walk in Weaverville, a book page that exists. Real does not mean oversharing his kids, his search for other remote roles, a client's books, or a struggle he has not decided to tell. Honest and bounded. If he has not lived it, it stays off the card.

### 12. Your website matters less than posting consistently

**Does not hold for the company site.** `peacemakersai.com` is how a buyer understands the Blueprint. Consistency does not replace that page. The consulting log already parked this line.

**For James.** He does not need a new personal website this week. He does need the company site left alone and kept accurate. Posting on LinkedIn or on `@buildingpeacemakers` is the personal habit. It is not a reason to neglect the page that explains the offer, and it is not a reason to paste the offer under every personal video.

### 13. Polish is the excuse people use to never press post

**Holds as a diagnosis.** The stall he should watch in himself is waiting to sound more impressive.

**For James.** The card is the cure. Four or five words he actually means, then walk. He still does not press post on a clip that names a client, shows a child, aims at Cirrus, or reads like a job hunt. "Press post" is not permission to skip his own review. A person approves anything that would be public. This playbook is not that approval.

### 14. Ignite people's emotions

**The feeling holds. The tactic does not.** She says it as a tip and the reactor immediately disagrees. What actually lands for James's readers is recognition: someone finally said the thing they have been living with. Anger, fear, and a rally are the wrong fuel. They also age badly next to a Cirrus job and a homeschool.

**For James.** Say the concrete thing and stop. "The close still happens in a spreadsheet next to the system you already own" will do more than a speech about how angry that should make someone. On the family account, a true sentence about the day is the whole post. No music swell, and no "you NEED to know this."

### In the same Reel, not her point: one skip-rate number is the only number

The man in the cap leaves her fourth tip and says the stall is not knowing what to track. He names skip rate on social, swipe rate on YouTube Shorts, and retention on TikTok, says a low skip and a high TikTok retention are the same number inverted, and says that is the only number.

**Flag. Do not run the brand on it.** The consulting log already checked the platforms. Instagram's own insight screen shows views, watch time, viewers, average watch time, and follows. Skip rate is a developer field (`reels_skip_rate`, first 3 seconds), not the number in the app. YouTube Shorts shows how many chose to view versus swiped away. It is not named "swipe rate," and it is not a full-video hold. TikTok shows average watch time, the share who watched the whole video, and where people drop off. TikTok's own tutorial says no single engagement metric is the one that matters. A 3-second skip and a full watch are related. They are not one official number flipped upside down.

**For James.** If a short is already up, read the figure that app puts on the screen: Instagram average watch time, YouTube "chose to view" versus swiped away, TikTok average watch time and the share who finished. Then stop. Do not install a skip-rate tool. Do not call one ratio the business. With no post up yet, the only count that matters this week is whether one card got filmed.

## Pillars

Four. Two example hooks each, in his voice. Hooks are starters for a card, not captions that have been approved.

### 1. After go-live — LinkedIn

The skill people should associate with his name. Ongoing NetSuite support for finance teams. Not implementations. Not a side offer.

- "Go-live is the week finance finds out what the system actually does."
- "If the close still sits in one person's head, the handoff is not done."

### 2. Use what you already have — LinkedIn, and a plainer version on `@buildingpeacemakers`

Same belief as the way he advises: fix the work and the tools already in the building before adding one. On LinkedIn the example is NetSuite (a saved search, a role, a close checklist). On the family account the example is one small job for an owner or a household. No tool logos for their own sake. No claim that AI saved a dollar figure.

- "I don't start with a new tool. I start with the step someone still does by hand."
- "If I can't explain it to the person who does the work, it doesn't go in."

### 3. The week after — occasional, either room, different story

The through-line, used sparingly so it does not become a veteran costume or a sermon. LinkedIn gets the finance version. The family account gets the home version. One story per post.

- "The part of the Army I still use is what had to work the week after the move."
- "Sustainment is a dull word. It is also why the books close."

### 4. Catholic homeschool — `@buildingpeacemakers` only

Faith and homeschool together. He records from a card, on a walk, unscripted. He does not show the kids. The pillars, the hooks, and this week's walks are in [`docs/homeschool-brand-playbook.md`](homeschool-brand-playbook.md). Publishing stays a mention when a real page is worth showing.

- "The kids stay off this camera. The work is at home."
- "I film from a card on a walk. If I haven't lived it, I don't say it."

**Not pillars.** Real estate. A certification series. Nonprofit as an identity. Book ads. "Day in the life." Anything that starts with "I'm excited to announce."

## Leave out, on purpose

- Any line that sounds like a job search, including soft ones ("exploring what's next," "open to remote").
- Client names, screenshots of a ledger, or a story a Cirrus client could recognize.
- Criticism of implementations, of Cirrus, or of the team that does that work.
- NetSuite help offered as a side practice.
- The 100k line, the "be rich" card, and any income, follower, or ROI claim.
- Her examples as his enemies, and the word "cult."
- The kids, including backs of heads, voices that identify them, and school details that point at them.
- A stitch, duet, or re-edit of this Reel.
- A skip-rate project.
